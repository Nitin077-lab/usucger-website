Finding funding for geotechnical engineering research

This page offers practical pointers to help early career geotechnical engineering faculty compete successfully for research funding.

📄 An updated guide is planned
The Council's earlier Do's and Don'ts funding guide has been retired because it was out of date. The Board is considering preparing an updated version; it will be posted here and announced on the USUCGER email list when available.

Where geotechnical research money comes from

USUCGER exists in part because of NSF's program in geomechanical, geotechnical and geoenvironmental engineering in the Directorate of Engineering — preserving that funding source was the reason the Council was founded in 1985. Advocacy for research funding at federal, state and industrial/private agencies remains the first of the Council's long-term objectives.

Beyond NSF, the community draws on state DOT research programs, federal agencies with infrastructure and hazards missions, and industry partners. Announcements of funding opportunities circulate on the USUCGER email list, so keeping your membership current is the simplest way to hear about them early.

Getting the most out of this

  • Read the program solicitation closely before you draft — most of the recurring problems are structural, not editorial.
  • Talk to a program officer early, before the proposal is written.
  • Ask a colleague who has served on a panel to read a draft. Panel experience is the single most useful thing to borrow.
  • Watch the list for calls, and give yourself more lead time than feels necessary.
